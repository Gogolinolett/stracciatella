# Problem

There is no smooth transition between tasks - the bot stands still for 60 ticks after every block break when more work is queued. Why?

# Lösung

Decision: when walkAfterCollect is set and taskQueue.peek() is not null, COLLECTING exits as soon as items are absent, bypassing the standard itemAbsenceTicks 60-tick sustained-absence window. When no further task is queued the original sustained-absence window applies. Companion change: if the bot ends up within reach of the next task by the time COLLECTING exits, for example the next ore in a vein right next to the one just mined, skip SCANNING and transition straight to LOOKING, because the aim cue is unnecessary when there is no movement to do. Alternatives: always wait itemAbsenceTicks (original) pro: tolerates transient absence between sequentially spawning drops; contra: adds a visible 60-tick pause after every block break in a multi-target flow, reported as no fluid transition between tasks. Walk toward the next task during COLLECTING (first attempt) pro: removes the pause and keeps the bot moving; contra: the walk is a dumb horizontal pursuit rather than PathWalker, so the bot bumps into the next target from the wrong angle, for example directly against the side of a tree trunk, and LOOKING then cannot establish line of sight so the chop-tree task fails with a look timeout - replacing the NAVIGATING standoff logic with a direct walk is wrong. Reduce itemAbsenceTicks globally pro: one-line change; contra: trades smoothness in multi-task flows against pickup reliability in single-task flows where multiple drops arrive in waves, and the 60-tick wait is the right answer for the single-task case. Skip the wait only when more work is queued (chosen) pro: a single surgical condition where queued-task flows get the immediate transition and single-task flows keep their robustness, with straggler drops on the walk path picked up by the 1.5-block vanilla radius during NAVIGATING; contra: the next task SCANNING to NAVIGATING sequence does the transition rather than continuing the walk, though the brief camera turn cue actually matches the human look where you are going pattern. Rationale: the itemAbsenceTicks wait solves a different problem (the multi-drop pickup race) than the smoothness complaint addresses (queue-driven multi-task flow); one window cannot satisfy both, and the queue gives a deterministic signal to switch.
