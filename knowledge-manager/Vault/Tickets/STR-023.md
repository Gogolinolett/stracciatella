# Problem

Per-tick traces show the smooth camera producing 141 degrees of yaw in a single tick - why does the spring snap on large turns?

# Lösung

Decision: updateYaw clamps the yaw angular velocity to MAX_YAW_SPEED_DEG_PER_TICK of 35 deg per tick, scaled by the look-speed multiplier. Alternatives: uncapped spring (original) pro: mathematically clean, converges fastest; contra: the spring first tick after a target change covers TURN_ACCEL times delta, so for a 180 deg flip that is a 144 deg single-tick snap. Per-tick yaw traces during pathwalking showed 74-141 deg jumps at path starts, at landing-brake retargets, and whenever a near node atan2 target flipped: the spring only smooths small deltas, and for large ones it IS the snap it was meant to prevent. Lower TURN_ACCEL globally pro: no new constant; contra: slows every turn including small corrections, so jump-facing waits (18-36 deg tolerance) and the whole tuned movement physics shift, while the problem is only the first saturated ticks of large turns. Velocity cap scaled by the look-speed multiplier (chosen) pro: large turns become a constant-rate sweep of about 35 deg per tick, roughly 700 deg per second, which is a brisk visibly continuous mouse turn that eases out via the unchanged spring once the remaining delta is small, and small-delta behaviour is byte-identical while multiplier scaling keeps per-target speed variance alive during the saturated phase; contra: adds 2-4 ticks to 90-180 deg turns and jump-facing fires correspondingly later, verified against the full jump-timing test suite. Cap inside consumers pro: camera module untouched; contra: every consumer would duplicate the clamp, and the failure mode lives in the shared spring rather than in any one consumer. Rationale: measured traces showed the smooth camera producing 141 deg in one tick, and the cap converts exactly those ticks into a sweep while leaving all small-delta smoothing untouched, in the one place all consumers share.
