# Problem

Dropped items are not always collected - why does the bot walk to an item and never pick it up?

# Lösung

Decision: tickCollecting selects the next item to walk toward by 3D distance from the player rather than horizontal-only, skips items whose absolute dy exceeds 4 blocks, and stops walking when the 3D distance drops below 1.5, the vanilla pickup radius. Alternatives: horizontal-only nearest with a 1.0-block walk stop (original) pro: simple; contra: an item 1 horizontal block away with dy=2, in the dug-out hole the bot just stood on, wins the nearest tie over an item 2 horizontal blocks away on the same Y; the bot walks to the in-hole item but its 3D distance is sqrt(5), about 2.24, which is outside the 1.5 vanilla pickup radius, so it never gets close enough - reported as dropped items are not always collected. 3D nearest with a dy filter of at most 4 and a 1.5 walk stop (chosen) pro: picks the item the bot can actually reach by horizontal walk, with the Y filter rejecting items 4 or more blocks above, unreachable without climbing, or below, past safe-fall range where the bot would take fall damage chasing them, and the 1.5 stop matches the server-side pickup radius so the bot stops exactly when vanilla pickup will fire; contra: does not help items truly stuck behind walls or in water, which need A* to a reachable standoff and are addressed separately. A* path to a mesh node near the item pro: handles every reachable item including around walls; contra: requires per-tick A* and a mesh covering the item neighbourhood, and the mesh may not exist for the immediate drop area - heavier than needed for the common case. Drop tracking with a predicted final position pro: simulates the bounce and walks to the predicted resting place; contra: the server bounces drops in ways the client cannot always predict, so the precision is wasted. Rationale: 3D plus filter closes the common failure case, a drop bouncing into the bot own dug hole, with three small one-line changes, and leaves the door open to layer A* on top later for truly hard cases.
