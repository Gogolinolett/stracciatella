# Problem

The bot starts mining before it is actually aiming at the right block, or keeps staring after the crosshair is already on it - what gates LOOKING to INTERACTING?

# Lösung

Decision: LOOKING transitions to INTERACTING the moment mc.hitResult is a BlockHitResult and its getBlockPos() equals the target, meaning the client raycast points at the target block. There is no angular-convergence requirement, and the camera keeps easing toward its aim point during INTERACTING. The pre-attack hesitation still sits between the gate firing and the first attack. There is also no cosmetic settle countdown after aim, and the tool-settle wait that used to open INTERACTING is gone, replaced by selecting the tool during LOOKING - the carried-item packet travels to the server in parallel with the smooth camera turn, and INTERACTING calls resendCarriedItem once on tick 1 as a dropped-packet safety net. Alternatives: angular tolerance only (original) pro: simple; contra: when an obstacle sits in the line of sight the camera can be within tolerance of the target vector while the raycast lands on the obstacle, so the bot transitions and visibly starts attacking the wrong block. Angular plus hit-result (previous) pro: hit-result is authoritative while the angular check throttles polling; contra: the angular gate delays the attack past the moment a human would click, since the crosshair is already on the block but the bot keeps staring until the camera is within 5 deg of the ideal aim point, and the throttle argument is void because mc.hitResult is a field read. Hit-result only (chosen) pro: fires exactly when a human would click, as the crosshair touches the block, with obstacle correctness living entirely in the hit-result check so a raycast on an obstacle holds the gate and lookTimeout fails cleanly, proven by the mine-blocked-by-obstacle test; contra: the carried-item packet window shrinks when the crosshair is already near the target at LOOKING entry, covered by the per-tick resend in LOOKING, the defensive resend on INTERACTING tick 1, and the fact that the server only computes drops at break completion several ticks later. Server-side reach check pro: authoritative; contra: rejection is silent and arrives after the visible mis-aim, addressing neither reported symptom. Rationale: both reported symptoms bracket the same truth - the crosshair-on-block raycast, not camera convergence, is when mining should begin, and the angular gate added latency without adding correctness once the hit-result check existed.
