# Problem

Which phase transitions should get a reaction delay, and why is the LOOKING to INTERACTING gate excluded?

# Lösung

Decision: a Gaussian reaction delay (mean 4 ticks, sigma 2, clamped to 1-10) is inserted at three transitions where the bot otherwise reacts instantly: SCANNING to NAVIGATING, POSITIONING to LOOKING, and INTERACTING-broken to LOOKING or next-LOOKING. The delay is not applied on the LOOKING to INTERACTING gate, which already uses the more authoritative pre-attack hesitation while the hit-result gate prevents the gate from firing too early. Alternatives: apply the reaction delay on every transition pro: most consistent humanness; contra: the LOOKING to INTERACTING gate is the latency-sensitive one for block-break timing in accelerated tests, and adding a second uncorrelated random delay on top of the pre-attack hesitation doubles the variance for no readability gain, since viewers cannot tell two short delays from one slightly longer one. No reaction delay anywhere, relying on camera smoothing pro: simplest; contra: camera smoothing only hides reaction time during a turn, and between phases - for example when the bot has already aimed and now decides to walk - there is nothing to mask the instant transition. Reaction delay only at top-of-task on SCANNING entry pro: one place to tune; contra: misses the most visible cue, the pause after a block breaks, because a bot that drops a log and instantly turns to the next log reads as a script while the pause is exactly the human notices the result moment. Reaction delay at the three chosen transitions (chosen) pro: captures the cues a viewer actually notices - decided to walk, decided to attack the next block, spotted the next block in reach - while avoiding stacking with the pre-attack hesitation; contra: the mechanism, a deferred Runnable plus a delay counter, is one extra state in the tick loop. Implementation note: the delay is realised by storing a Runnable and a tick countdown on BotController, and while the countdown is non-zero the main tick early-returns without running the current phase logic or incrementing phaseTicks, so per-phase timeouts such as navigateTimeout and lookTimeout are not eaten by the delay. releaseMovementKeys() is called when scheduling so the bot visibly stops moving during the beat instead of coasting.
