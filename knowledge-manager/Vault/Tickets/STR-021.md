# Problem

How is per-target variance in look speed introduced without destabilising the spring?

# Lösung

Decision: CameraController.setLookSpeedMultiplier(double m) scales the spring acceleration on both yaw and pitch, while friction is held constant. The default is 1.0, which is critical damping on yaw. Alternatives: scale acceleration only (chosen) pro: a simple knob where m above 1 introduces mild overshoot and m below 1 slows convergence, both of which feel human, and it preserves the soft feel of the damper; contra: underdamping at high m could in theory oscillate, but the multiplier range of roughly 0.7-1.3 stays well within stable bounds. Scale both acceleration and friction pro: stays critically damped at every multiplier; contra: removes the variance of behaviour so every aim feels the same shape only faster or slower, losing the sometimes the camera overshoots a hair texture that reads as human. Replace constants per target pro: most flexibility; contra: couples consumers to physics, as they would have to know which values are stable. Rationale: the overshoot at m above 1 is exactly the human-like artefact wanted - a perfectly damped camera at every speed still feels like a machine, just at different speeds. The bot rolls m from lookSpeedMin/Max (default 0.7 to 1.3) per target. See also [[STR-017]].
