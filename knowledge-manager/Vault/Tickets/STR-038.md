# Problem

Path traces show the walk going straight at the target and then dog-legging sideways for the last two nodes - how is the standoff node chosen?

# Lösung

Decision: findStandoffNode scores candidate nodes that are in reach of the target by 1.05 times distToPlayer plus horizDistToTarget and takes the minimum. By the triangle inequality the sum is minimal for nodes on the straight player-to-target line, and the slight walk-distance bias makes the winner the first in-reach node on that line, which is also deterministic since all line nodes share the same raw sum and an unbiased score would fall back to hash iteration order. Alternatives: player-nearest in-reach node (original) pro: shortest walk; contra: regularly picks a node one block to the side of the approach line, whichever in-reach node happens to be closest to where the player stands, and debug path traces showed the walk going straight at the target and then dog-legging sideways for the last two nodes - the reported weird pathfinding in the ore-to-tree test. No human side-steps right before a target. Line-biased score (chosen) pro: walks read as straight at the target, stop in front of it, at the cost of one extra multiply per node; contra: the chosen node can be about 5 percent farther than the absolute nearest, which is irrelevant at reach distances. Target-nearest in-reach node pro: also line-ish; contra: walks past the natural stopping point right up to the block, which reads overeager and risks bumping into the target. Rationale: the dog-leg was a pure artifact of the scoring rather than of the mesh or A* - the path was the true shortest route to a badly chosen destination.
