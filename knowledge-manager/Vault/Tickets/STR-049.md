# Problem

How is an ore next to the tunnel acquired, and why did the thin line-of-sight approach fail?

# Lösung

Decision: oreScanRadius defaults to mining reach (4). A found ore is acquired by digging a 4-connected line of 1x2 columns, foot plus head at tunnel height, from the player column to the ore column, then the ore itself. Ores from one below the gallery floor, mined from above, to one above its ceiling, mined from below, are reachable; out-of-layer ores and galleries crossing bedrock or liquid go to the skip-set. Every planned block is recorded in an excavated set so the tunnel cave check never mistakes the run own galleries for natural caves. Alternatives: thin eye-to-ore line-of-sight exposure (first attempt) pro: fewest blocks; contra: failed in practice, because the line is computed from the planning position but deeper line blocks stay hidden behind the still-solid foot blocks, so the hit-result gate raycast lands on the blocker - 18 look timeouts in one test run - and the player cannot follow a non-walkable pinhole; its diggings also turned future tunnel slices to air, which the cave check then mis-read as cave ahead and failed the run. Large scan radius plus navigate to the ore pro: finds more diamonds per tunnel; contra: navigation needs mesh nodes, which do not exist inside solid rock, so reaching distant ore means excavating access tunnels, a planning problem far beyond v1, and it drags the bot away from the protected tunnel into unchecked terrain. Walkable 1x2 gallery plus excavated set (chosen) pro: each column is frontally visible from the previous one and the collect-walk pulls the player in after every block, so the hit-result gate always sees its target up close; 4-connected with one axis step per column because a diagonal line leaves corner walls the player cannot pass; and the excavated set cleanly separates our air from cave air; contra: roughly twice the blocks of a thin line, and it misses ore more than about 4 blocks off the tunnel and outside the 4 reachable layers - by design, since strip-mining density comes from tunnel length. Mine a 1x1 access shaft to each ore pro: reaches anything; contra: leaves the floor and ceiling riddled with holes the collect-walk can fall into, and is not walkable, with the same visibility problem as the thin line. Rationale: the gallery was chosen because the thin-line variant demonstrably fails on the two mechanics everything else relies on, hit-result visibility and collect-walk advancement, while the gallery rides exactly those mechanics - it is the shape a human digs to an ore spotted in the wall.
