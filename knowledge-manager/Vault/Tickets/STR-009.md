# Problem

After mining a wall the next A* path still ignores the hole and drives the bot back through the cleared section - when is the chunk mesh invalidated?

# Lösung

Decision: LevelChunkMixin.setBlockState invalidates the mesh for the chunk via MeshManager.invalidateMesh(chunkCoord) whenever the change flips air to solid or back, the chunk level is the client one, and at least one entity has a mesh for that chunk. Alternatives: empty mixin with no invalidation (original) pro: simple, no perf cost; contra: mining a wall leaves the mesh thinking the wall is still solid, the next A* search finds a path that ignores the hole the bot just dug, and PathWalker drives the bot back through the no-longer-walkable section. Per-block slice update pro: cheap per update; contra: tricky to get right because a single block change can affect not just its own column but every neighbour reachability (line-of-sight checks span 5 blocks either way), so the bug surface is high while full regen is correctness-first. Full chunk regen on every block update without a filter pro: trivially correct; contra: every redstone tick, leaf decay and waterlogged toggle would re-walk the entire 16x16 chunk and tests would slow to a crawl. Full chunk regen filtered to air/solid flips plus active-mesh plus client-side (chosen) pro: three short and cheap conditions prune the expensive call to the cases where it matters, non-mesh chunks pay nothing, and the level filter prevents the double-fire in single-player; contra: sub-state edits that do affect walkability, such as a stair direction change altering the upper face slope, are not currently detected - not a problem in practice since bots do not navigate stairs as a special case yet. Lazy dirty flag with regen on next access pro: avoids work for chunks not currently queried; contra: adds a flag to track and a check on every findOrBuildNearestNode call, and the current filters already make eager regen affordable. Rationale: the filters reduce the firing rate by more than 100x since most setBlockState calls are sub-state edits or in chunks no bot is using, a 16x16 walk is sub-millisecond when it does fire, and the resulting mesh is guaranteed consistent with what the client sees. The lazy-flag option remains available if profiling later shows a bottleneck.
