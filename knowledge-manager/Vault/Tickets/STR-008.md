# Problem

Why does the bot whip its camera around to face the travel direction just to brake after a jump landing?

# Lösung

Decision: during the active landing brake (speed above 0.1 b/t after a jump landing) the camera keeps turning smoothly toward the next path target via camera.updateYaw with no snap, and the residual momentum is countered with movement keys - the momentum direction relative to the current view yaw is quantized to the 8 key directions (max 22.5 deg error) and the opposing combo is pressed: S when the momentum runs forward, A/D counter-strafe when it runs sideways, W when it runs backward, diagonals combined. Alternatives: snapYaw(velocityYaw) plus press S (original) pro: brake force exactly antiparallel to momentum, fastest possible deceleration; contra: the camera visibly whips around to face the travel direction just to brake and then has to turn back, reported as the bot turning around to brake instead of just pressing S - an instant yaw snap plus an unmotivated turn is a double bot-tell. Keep the view on target and always press S pro: single-key simplicity, no snap; contra: S pushes opposite the view, not opposite the momentum, so after diagonal jumps or mid-turn the momentum can run sideways to the view, S then barely decelerates (cos near 0 at 90 deg) and adds a sideways push that can shove the player off a single-block platform. 8-way key counter-brake with smooth view (chosen) pro: reads exactly like a player braking with eyes already on the next target, worst-case key misalignment is 22.5 deg so at least 92 percent of the push still decelerates; contra: slightly weaker than a perfect antiparallel brake, up to 38 percent of the push acts sideways for a few ticks, verified against the single-block corner tests. 4-way quantization pro: simpler sector logic; contra: max misalignment 45 deg, only about 71 percent decelerates and up to 71 percent acts sideways, measurably worse on single-block landings for no readability gain. Rationale: the brake purpose - kill momentum before the next segment - survives quantization, while the camera behaviour, the only part the user actually sees, becomes indistinguishable from a player: gaze on the next target, fingers doing the braking.
