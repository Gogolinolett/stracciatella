# Problem

The bot walks through its drops staring straight ahead, and when it does track them it cranes its head further and further down - how should the COLLECTING gaze work?

# Lösung

Decision: during COLLECTING the camera aims at what the bot is doing - the nearest item while walking to it (walkToward takes the full 3D target), the item while standing in pickup range unless it is nearly underfoot (horizontal distance squared at most 0.5), lastMinedPos while waiting for the drop to sync, and the last aim point (still saccading) while waiting out the absence window. All drop-directed aims go through aimCollectGaze, where the downward pitch is capped at a per-phase angle drawn uniformly from collectGazePitchMinDeg to collectGazePitchMaxDeg (default 38-52 deg). When the direct angle to the item would exceed the cap, the aim point is pushed out along the same horizontal direction to the distance where the pitch equals the cap, keeping the same yaw so walk steering is unaffected while the gaze rests on the ground ahead. Micro-saccades are enabled for the whole phase. Alternatives: smooth yaw to walk direction only (original) pro: steers the walk correctly; contra: pitch stays frozen at whatever INTERACTING left, namely eye level at the mined block, so the bot walks through its drops staring ahead and stands dead still with a locked gaze during the 60-tick absence window - both read as bot. Aim at the item point directly (first attempt) pro: the gaze follows the drop; contra: the pitch steepens continuously on approach, about 22 deg at 4 blocks and about 58 deg at 1 block, so the head visibly cranes further down, which the user reported as unnatural - humans do not head-track a point on the floor, they hold a scanning angle. Aim at the item with pitch capped at a ground-scan angle (chosen) pro: far away the bot looks at the drop at a flat angle, and closing in it holds a steady scanning the floor ahead posture while the drop enters the lower view, with per-phase variance of 38-52 deg avoiding a fixed signature angle and yaw unchanged so walking is unaffected; contra: the gaze passes over items closer than the cap distance of about 1.4 blocks, which is acceptable since vanilla pickup needs no aim and this is exactly the floor-scan look requested. Fixed 45 deg pitch during COLLECTING with no item tracking pro: simplest; contra: loses the distant-drop glance that makes the walk-over read intentional, and pins the pitch even when the drop is above eye level on a ledge, where looking up at it is the natural move - the cap only restricts downward pitch. Rationale: the walk steering needs item-directed yaw anyway, and capping only the downward pitch keeps both stories right: I see the drop over there at range, and I am scanning the ground in front of me up close.
