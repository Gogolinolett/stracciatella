# Problem

A target that is in reach but has no line of sight fails forever with repeated look timeouts at identical positions - what should happen on a look timeout?

# Lösung

Decision: when LOOKING times out, meaning the crosshair raycast never reached the target, the first timeout per target does not fail the task - it sets forceApproach and transitions to POSITIONING, which walks until the player is within APPROACH_CLOSE_DISTANCE (2.0) of the target instead of stopping at reach distance, then retries LOOKING. The second timeout fails with diagnostics covering position, chosen face and actual hitResult. Companion change: waiting out the full lookTimeout (40 ticks, 2 s at real speed) before re-approaching reads as staring, so once the camera has settled on its aim point (angular at most 3 deg) and the crosshair has rested on the same wrong block for WRONG_HIT_STREAK_TICKS (8) consecutive ticks, the geometry is not going to change and the re-approach fires immediately; the full timeout remains the fallback for non-block hit results such as sky, and as the second, failing attempt. Alternatives: fail on the first look timeout (original) pro: simple; contra: the controller had no in reach but no line of sight so move path, because POSITIONING stops at reach distance 4, so a target visible only from close up - for example a gallery block below eye level whose ceiling blocks the ray from a staircase ledge - failed forever no matter how often the planner retried, observed as repeated look timeouts at identical player positions in the miner. A human just steps closer. Re-approach once then fail (chosen) pro: fixes the systematic viewpoint problem for every consumer including bot commands and behaviors, and is bounded to one extra POSITIONING walk per target with the second timeout still failing cleanly, while the obstacle-blocked case keeps failing since walking into the wall never reaches close range; contra: adds roughly 40-100 ticks before a genuinely impossible target fails. Planner-side position gates, tried in the miner first pro: no controller change; contra: whack-a-mole, because the player position at plan time differs from execution time due to collect-walks and edge-standing on stair lips, so the planner cannot guarantee the viewpoint - the execution layer is the only place that knows the look failed. Raycast visibility pre-check before enqueuing pro: catches it earlier; contra: duplicates what LOOKING already discovers, and the answer to from where still requires moving, which is exactly the re-approach. Rationale: step closer when you cannot see it is both the human behavior and the only fix that works at the layer where visibility is actually determined. See also [[STR-050]].
