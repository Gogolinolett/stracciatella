# Problem

Why are staircase steps 3 blocks high, and what happens when a step is already fully open?

# Lösung

Decision: DESCEND digs 1 step per batch - foot, head and ceiling clearance, so 3 blocks - at one forward and one down from the cursor, requiring a solid block below the new foot. A step that is already fully open, all three air, fails the run as cave ahead during descent. Alternatives: 2-block steps with foot plus head only pro: fewer blocks; contra: the ceiling block at the old head height clips the player head as they walk down the 1-block drop and they get stuck on the lip - 3 high is the minimum for walking down a 1-deep step. Vertical shaft with pillar down pro: fastest descent; contra: digging straight down is the canonical way to die, and the player cannot follow a vertical shaft via collect-walk. Staircase tolerating all-air steps, walking down through caves pro: keeps going; contra: descending into an unlit cave without combat or lighting support is a session-loss generator, and fail-fast matches the hazard policy - partial-air steps with 1-2 blocks already open are still dug normally. Rationale: the 3-block staircase is the shape a careful human digs, every block of it is verified solid-or-cleared before the player walks it, and the collect-walk naturally pulls the player down each finished step.
