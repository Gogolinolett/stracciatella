# Problem

Why does the bot turn around on the target block after landing instead of simply stopping where it lands?

# Lösung

Decision: the final path node counts as reached when the player is on the ground within FINAL_ARRIVAL_RADIUS (0.5) of the true block center, with the per-node random target offset excluded, and horizontal speed is at most FINAL_ARRIVAL_MAX_SPEED (0.12 b/t). Intermediate nodes keep the tight sphere (0.18 against the offset target) or the in-bounds box check. Alternatives: tight sphere only (original) pro: guarantees a centered stop comfortably inside the 0.6 test arrival radius; contra: the bot lands off-center (jumps always do, and the walk target itself sits up to 0.25 off-center from the random offset) and then walks the last fraction of a block, at which point the desired yaw flips sign whenever the player oversteps and the overshoot logic spins him back - the reported turning around the block center after landing. Box check for the final node too pro: simplest unification; contra: triggers up to 0.65 from center, outside the downstream 0.6 test radius, and standing half-off the block edge is not a finished-looking stop. 0.5 true-center radius plus low-speed gate (chosen) pro: fires the moment the bot stands securely on the block, covering every normal landing and the offset walk target so no re-centering walk ever starts, and the whole 0.5 disc counts as arrived so the yaw-flip pirouette cannot occur; contra: the resting point varies by up to 0.5 from center, which is exactly the human-looking outcome and is tolerated by all downstream checks (tests 0.6, WalkTravelMethod 2 blocks, bot reach 4.0). Velocity-projected arrival pro: theoretically earliest stop; contra: predicting the friction slide duplicates physics the brake logic already handles. Rationale: the pirouette is purely a product of chasing a sub-0.25-block point - widening arrived to the area a human would consider being on the block makes the pathological geometry disappear, while the speed gate preserves the only thing the tight stop actually protected, not sliding off the far edge.
